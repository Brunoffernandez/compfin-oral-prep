\hypertarget{the-task-in-my-own-words}{%
\subsection{The task (in my own words)}\label{the-task-in-my-own-words}}

Price a two-asset European \textbf{basket call} under the risk-neutral
measure Q. The basket is \texttt{B\_T\ =\ w1\ S1(T)\ +\ w2\ S2(T)}; the
payoff is \texttt{max(B\_T\ -\ K,\ 0)}, discounted at
\texttt{e\^{}\{-rT\}}.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Q1 --- Monte Carlo.} Simulate the basket and report the price
  with a standard error / 95\% CI.

  \begin{enumerate}
  \def\labelenumi{(\alph{enumi})}
  \tightlist
  \item
    both assets GBM, correlated, exact terminal simulation. (b)
    \texttt{S1} GBM, \texttt{S2} a CIR square-root process under the
    Q-drift \texttt{r\ S2}; demonstrate (i) the weak /
    time-discretization convergence of the Euler scheme and (ii) the
    statistical \texttt{O(1/sqrt(M))} convergence.
  \end{enumerate}
\item
  \textbf{Q2 --- Moment matching.} Compute the raw moments of
  \texttt{B\_T} in closed form, then approximate the basket distribution
  by matching 2, 3, 4 moments (log-normal, shifted log-normal, Johnson
  SU) and price the call from each. Benchmark against a high-precision
  Monte Carlo price.
\item
  \textbf{Q3 --- 2D COS.} Price the basket with the two-dimensional COS
  method (Ruijter \& Oosterlee 2012), expanding the joint density of the
  two state variables on \texttt{{[}a1,b1{]}\ x\ {[}a2,b2{]}},
  exploiting \texttt{rho=0} so the 2D density coefficients factorise
  into a product of 1D coefficients. Show convergence in \texttt{N}.
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{my-approach-and-why}{%
\subsection{My approach and why}\label{my-approach-and-why}}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Q1:} exact GBM simulation at maturity where possible (no time
  grid needed); Euler--Maruyama with full truncation for the CIR leg
  because exact joint simulation is unavailable when
  \texttt{rho\ !=\ 0}.
\item
  \textbf{Q2:} derive \texttt{E{[}B\_T\^{}k{]}} analytically from the
  binomial expansion of \texttt{(w1\ S1\ +\ w2\ S2)\^{}k} and the cross
  moments of correlated log-normals (and the non-central chi-square
  moments for the CIR leg), then fit successively richer distributions
  to more moments.
\item
  \textbf{Q3:} 1D characteristic functions per marginal, cumulant-rule
  truncation range per marginal, payoff coefficients by 2D trapezoidal
  quadrature, assembled as \texttt{chi1\^{}T\ V\ chi2}.
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{the-hardest-question-fang-could-ask-here-and-my-answer}{%
\subsection{The hardest question Fang could ask here --- and my
answer}\label{the-hardest-question-fang-could-ask-here-and-my-answer}}

\textbf{Most likely deep-dive (highest probability): the COS truncation
range \texttt{{[}a,b{]}}.} Fang co-invented COS; the cumulant truncation
rule is her result, and this assignment had a real bug there. She will
go straight to \textbf{\texttt{Q3\_basket\_COS\ (1).py:65-71}}
(\texttt{cumulant\_range}) and its call sites \textbf{\texttt{:82-83}
(Q3a)} and \textbf{\texttt{:125-127} (Q3b)}.

What the code factually does: \texttt{cumulant\_range} returns
\texttt{{[}k1\ -\ L*sqrt(\textbar{}k2\textbar{}\ +\ sqrt(\textbar{}k4\textbar{})),\ k1\ +\ L*sqrt(\textbar{}k2\textbar{}\ +\ sqrt(\textbar{}k4\textbar{})){]}}
(\texttt{Q3\_basket\_COS\ (1).py:70-71}), with \texttt{L\ =\ 12}. For a
normal marginal \texttt{k4\ =\ 0}, so the half-width is
\texttt{L*sqrt(variance)} (\texttt{:82-83}). For the CIR leg, the
half-width uses the non-central chi-square cumulants
\texttt{k2\ =\ 4\ c\^{}2\ lambda}, \texttt{k4\ =\ 192\ c\^{}4\ lambda}
(\texttt{:123-124}), and the lower end is clamped to \texttt{0}
(\texttt{:127}).

\textbf{The bug (special focus): a too-small \texttt{c2} made
\texttt{{[}a,b{]}} too narrow at short maturities.} The half-width
scales like \texttt{sqrt(k2)}, and \texttt{k2} is the variance term ---
for a normal marginal \texttt{k2\ =\ sigma\^{}2\ T} (\texttt{:82-83},
\texttt{:125}), which \textbf{shrinks with \texttt{T}}. At short
maturity \texttt{c2} is small, so
\texttt{width\ =\ L*sqrt(c2\ +\ sqrt(c4))}
(\texttt{Q3\_basket\_COS\ (1).py:70}) collapses and \texttt{{[}a,b{]}}
becomes too narrow to contain the support of the density / payoff,
truncating mass and corrupting the price. The fix lives at the range
construction --- the \texttt{cumulant\_range} formula at
\textbf{\texttt{:70}} and the \texttt{L} value at \textbf{\texttt{:65}}
(and the non-negativity clamp at \texttt{:127}).
